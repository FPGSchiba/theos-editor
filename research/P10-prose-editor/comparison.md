## Scoring rules

- **Scale** (spec §4.3): 0 = not possible, no credible path · 1 = possible with heavy custom work or known open bugs · 2 = works with moderate custom work · 3 = works with stock features and little glue. Licence: `pass` or `fail`. Weighted total = Σ weight × score, at most 54. A 0 on criterion 1 or 2, or a licence `fail`, makes the total `knockout`.
- **The weakest part decides.** Most criteria list several things (criterion 8 lists seven). A cell gets the score of the weakest of them, because the criterion works only when all of it works. The section names the strong parts, but they do not lift the cell.
- **Live preview.** Write mode is a live preview (Jann's choice, spec §3): the Markdown of the construct under the caret shows raw and everything else is rendered. Link chips are atomic: one arrow press steps over a chip, one Backspace after it removes the whole link, and a link shows raw only while it is being typed. Criteria 3–5 are scored for this mode.
- **Bytes are scored once.** Criterion 1 is judged at span level (G5: any edit leaves untouched bytes identical; block level is not enough). For a tree editor, the best-effort path that spec §4.5 plans (untouched blocks keep their source text, change steps are mapped onto the source as span edits) counts as a credible path, so no tree editor is knocked out on paper; the cell scores what that path costs. Criterion 5 asks that editing a parameter changes only its line; whether the saved bytes outside that line stay identical is criterion 1's question, so criterion 5 judges whether the edit stays in that line's text or node.
- **Front matter** (criterion 2). In Write mode an adapter may split the front matter off and re-attach it on save. What separates candidates is Source mode: the same component shows the whole file raw with the front matter protected (up to 3), or Source mode needs a second component (at most 2). The front matter bytes must stay identical through typing, delete at the body start, select-all and type, paste, and undo.
- **Maintenance** (criterion 10) reads the scale as the cost of staying on the component: 3 = little effort, no material risk in releases, backing, open issues in our areas or API stability · 2 = material risks that pinning versions and watching the tracker can manage · 1 = risks that would need our own patches or workarounds kept for good · 0 = unmaintained. One risk is common to every candidate that writes the file from a tree (ProseMirror/Tiptap, Milkdown, Lexical): releases that change the Markdown output. All three ship such changes in minor or patch releases, so the risk is scored the same for all three. Pinned versions catch such a change at an upgrade, and spec §5 recommends that P12 port P10's byte-test method, which would show it; so it counts as a risk that pinning and watching can manage.
- **Milkdown's Crepe.** A Theos editor would be built on the headless `@milkdown/kit`, not on the ready-made features of `@milkdown/crepe` (LaTeX, list-item blocks, tables, AI). Issues that only those features show are named but set aside, in every criterion alike.
- **Evidence.** A source marked "researcher probe, not reproduced; supporting evidence only" (the researcher's own headless runs in Task 3) may support a score but never decides one: every cell cites at least one other source. The deciding measurements come later from the P10 harness. Forum posts by users (for example on the Obsidian forum) carry the weight of a user statement only. Where a dossier found nothing, the text says so; an absence is not evidence that something works.
- **Webviews.** Facts about spell check and IME in WebView2, WKWebView and WebKitGTK apply to every `contenteditable` candidate alike. They are listed once under Webviews and cited from each criterion 6 cell; they do not move one candidate against another.
- **Measured scores.** The rules above hold for the measured scores of the top two as well; how spec §4.4 is applied to the measurements is under Measured scores.

## CodeMirror 6

Researched: @codemirror/view 6.43.15, @codemirror/state 6.7.6, @codemirror/commands 6.11.1, @codemirror/language 6.13.1, @codemirror/lang-markdown 6.5.2, @codemirror/autocomplete 6.20.4, @codemirror/lint 6.9.7, @codemirror/merge 6.12.3, @lezer/markdown 1.8.1 [S112]. Dossier: [S132]. Measured: variant `codemirror` of the P10 harness (Tasks 8–11), run of 2026-11-03 [S242]. The prototype uses `@codemirror/view`, `state`, `commands`, `language` and `@lezer/markdown` at the versions above; `lang-markdown`, `autocomplete`, `lint` and `merge` were not needed [S247].

**Byte-stable Markdown: 3→3.** Measured as on paper. Every unedited corpus file came back identical, 17 of 17 (the CRLF file as its LF form), and every edit matched the oracle, 60 of 60, through `apply` and through real input, in Chromium and in WebKit [S242]. The composition and dead-key rows of the input report compare the saved bytes as well and match in Chromium [S244]. That answers, for compositions through the DevTools protocol, the paper's open question of how tight the range is that the view reads back from the DOM; WebKit could not compose (spec §4.7) [S244]. The adapter writes nothing of its own: `text()` is the document as it stands [S249]. 3.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 3.* The source text is the document. The guide says CodeMirror "treats the document as a flat string", stored as a tree of lines with UTF-16 offsets [S1], and the Lezer Markdown parser only builds a syntax tree for styling; its README says it only parses the document and does not help with output [S2]. Edits are change sets on that string and undo applies their inverses, so nothing is serialized again and bytes outside an edit stay as they were by construction [S3, S21]. The one normalization is line ends: by default CRLF and CR come back as LF [S3], which is the expected output for the corpus's CRLF file (spec §4.6); `EditorState.lineSeparator` would keep CRLF [S3]. The commands that add bytes are opt-in or can be switched off: `markdown()` installs list-continuing Enter, markup-deleting Backspace, HTML tag completion and `pasteURLAsLink`, each with an off switch [S4]. A probe read an LF file and a BOM back identically and confirmed the line-end behaviour [S133]. Left for the byte oracle: the view finds a user edit by reading the DOM and reporting the changed range, and how tight that range is for autocorrect, spell-check replacement and IME commits is not documented [S20]. Stock model, configuration only: 3.

**Front matter untouched: 3→3.** Measured as on paper, with another stock hook than the dossier named. `F-frontmatter-hidden` and `F-source-mode` pass in both engines, and every round-trip row at the boundary is identical: Backspace at the body start, paste at the body start, both select-all-and-type edits and undo back to the start [S243, S242]. Source mode is the same view: one compartment swaps the Write and Source extensions, and text, history and selection carry over [S249]. The stock `changeFilter` that the dossier named was not enough: measured, it lets an insertion at offset 0 through and loses the typed text of a select-all-and-type. The prototype uses a `transactionFilter` instead, which drops a change inside the front matter and starts a change that crosses it at the body start [S249]. That is the documented general hook, so the work is still stock. The front matter costs 109 lines and the modes 35 [S248]; most of it hides the front matter in Write mode (one block replace, an atomic range, a selection filter) and handles two edge cases, the caret at load and front matter that ends the file [S249]. Same component, stock hooks, a short filter: 3.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 3.* Source mode is the same view: `Compartment.reconfigure` swaps the Write-mode extensions for the Source-mode ones in one transaction, without re-creating the view [S3]. Protection is a documented hook: `EditorState.changeFilter` suppresses changes inside given ranges, and a transaction filter can rewrite any transaction [S3]; in Write mode a replace decoration from a state field hides the front matter [S20]. The glue is one filter function. A probe showed why the plain range form is not enough (an insertion at offset 0 passes, and select-all-and-type keeps the front matter but loses the typed text) and that a transaction filter that clamps every change to start after the front matter handles typing at 0, delete at the body start and select-all-and-type [S133]. Undo and redo run with `filter: false`, so no filter sees them [S21]. That is harmless while the front matter is protected in both modes, because undo only inverts changes that already passed the filter; it becomes a risk only if P12 lets Source mode edit the front matter (spec §4.5 leaves that to P12). Same component, stock hooks, a short filter: 3.

**Inline link chips: 2→2.** All seven link checks pass in both engines: the chip, the alias, the broken state, atomic stepping and deletion, the popover in prose, its kind filter in an attribute line, and Escape [S243]. Inserting through the popover and deleting a chip are byte-identical through both drivers [S242]. A chip is a `Decoration.replace` widget that mounts the shared `LinkChip`, found by a scan of the visible lines; the same set is the `atomicRanges`; a state field follows the typed `[[query`, and the popover is mounted on the page body [S249]. Chips appear in prose, callout titles and beat attribute lines [S249]. In a voice title they show only while the title is raw: off the caret the shared `VoiceHead` shows the speaker's title, not chips, as in ProseMirror, which uses the same component [S249, S250]. Links cost 203 lines, plus 68 for the typing state [S248]. Moderate custom code on stock mechanisms, as on paper: 2. Not 3, because none of it is a stock chip. The recorded limits are outside what the criterion defines: undoing a pick does not reopen the popover, and a composition inside `[[query` was not tried [S249].

*On paper (2026-10-31): 2.* Every part is a stock mechanism that needs Theos code. Parsing: `MarkdownConfig.parseInline` adds a `[[…]]` node; the bundled `Emoji` extension is a 10-line example [S2]. An npm search found no packaged wikilink extension [S134]. Rendering: `Decoration.replace({widget})` over the link; the view draws the widget as `contenteditable="false"` with a hidden widget buffer beside it [S20]. Atomic behaviour: passing the same decoration set to `EditorView.atomicRanges` makes cursor motion skip the chip, and `deleteCharBackward` and `deleteCharForward` widen a deletion to the whole range [S20, S21]. The atomic-range issues found are all closed (#1606, #978, #939, #923, #979) [S33]. Popover: `@codemirror/autocomplete` calls a completion source with the state at the caret, so the kinds allowed at that place are filtered there [S32]. Its list DOM belongs to the library, so the design system's LinkAutocomplete needs a custom `showTooltip` tooltip with its own key handling [S32, S20]. Broken state: the decoration reads the index from a state field [S20]. Raw while typed, chip once picked: the same state field rebuilds decorations from the selection [S20]. The one open parser issue, precedence next to `!` (lezer-parser/markdown #42), concerns `![[…]]`, which the criteria do not need [S135]. Obsidian users report IME trouble right after a link in its CodeMirror-based Live Preview; that is a user statement, weighed under criterion 6 [S136]. Moderate custom work (parser, decoration field, completion source, tooltip, Svelte widget) and no open defect in the mechanisms: 2. Not 3, because nothing here is a stock chip.

**Callouts with their own look: 2→2.** `F-voice`, `F-design`, `F-note` and `F-dev-word` pass in both engines, and the note-state edits and the sentence typed at the end of a voice block are byte-identical [S243, S242]. A callout is a run of lines from the Lezer tree with line decorations; the voice and note heads are widgets that mount the shared heads; the `> ` marks are hidden on the lines no selection touches; developer words are marks [S249]. 359 lines [S248]. Moderate custom code: 2. Recorded limits: Enter does not continue the `> ` marker, so a new line is a lazy line of the same paragraph until the author types `> `. Markdown keeps that line in the callout and its bytes are as typed, so it is a missing convenience, not a defect. A nested quote is drawn as one callout line, and callouts inside lists are not recognised; neither is in the data model [S249]. A `Design note:` line inside a callout is callout text: its developer words are skipped, but it gets no `tu-note` [S249]. The data model allows such a line inside in-world text, and voice blocks are in-world text, so it is a data-model construct; ProseMirror has the mirror case (a `Design note:` line right after prose lines), the corpus holds neither, and neither lowers a cell. Enter in a callout got no review probing in this prototype, unlike ProseMirror's three rounds, so these records may be incomplete.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 2.* A callout stays lines of ordinary text, so its text is editable in place without extra work [S20]. The look comes from stock decorations: `Decoration.line` classes per line, `Decoration.replace` to hide the `> ` marks off the caret line, and `EditorView.blockWrappers` (since 6.39.0) to wrap the callout's lines in one element with its own tag and attributes [S20]. The `[!voice]` marker needs a custom inline parser. Inline parsers cannot see their parent block, so the decoration pass checks for a `Blockquote` ancestor [S2, S133]. `Design note:` lines fit `MatchDecorator`. Developer words in voice text need the block context, which a view plugin over the syntax tree has (the pattern of Obsidian's decoration docs) [S20, S47]. The voice head (speaker title, beliefs, tier) is a widget on the title line while the caret is elsewhere [S20]. Block wrappers are young: composition bugs inside them were fixed in 6.39.15 and 6.42.1 [S20]. Moderate custom work on stock mechanisms: 2.

**Beat headings with attribute lines: 2→2.** `F-beat-heading`, `F-beat-row`, `F-beat-finding` and `F-live-preview` pass in both engines, and all six beat-parameter edits are byte-identical through both drivers [S243, S242]. The heading is an inline replace, the row a block widget from a state field that mounts the shared `BeatRow`, and the raw lines come back while a selection touches them; an edit through the row is one change of that line's value [S249]. 198 lines [S248]. Moderate custom code: 2. Recorded limits: a row that is drawn again (a new context, an edit in its lines) loses an open input, the focus does not come back after a commit, and an unknown key is shown but cannot be edited from the row [S249]. The two input-driver rows of beats 4 and 5 that failed after Task 11 were a harness driver, fixed by controller ruling 22 before the measurement run; the run has both identical [S249, S242].

*On paper (2026-10-31): 2.* A custom block parser turns `key:: value` lines into attribute nodes and parses links inside the values [S2, S133]. Editing one parameter changes only its line, because each attribute is one line of the source text [S1]. The hard part is showing several lines as one `tu-beat` row with chips. Line decorations style each line separately, so the row is a block replace widget over the attribute lines while the caret is outside them, and the raw lines while it is inside, which is live preview as specified; block widgets must come from a state field [S20]. Editing through the row means the widget dispatches a change to that line's value range [S3]. Findings: `@codemirror/lint` diagnostics or decorations, pushed with a transaction [S55]. The known risk of the technique is caret placement around rendered blocks: codemirror-rich-markdoc lists mismatched cursor positions after clicks into rendered blocks, and up/down landing at the wrong end, as known issues [S56]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Spell check and IME: 2→1.** Measured 2 at first (Task 18), 1 since controller ruling 32 (2026-11-04), on code reading that the harness did not check. Prose is real text in a `contenteditable` with `spellcheck` on: `contentAttributes` sets it on the content element [S258, S244]. It is off where a component draws: the chips, which stand in for the ids also on raw lines, the heads and the rendered beat row carry `spellcheck="false"`, and the code sets it on the design label [S244, S257, S259, S249]. But a raw attribute line, the live-preview view of the attribute lines while the caret is on them, is a plain `.cm-line` and inherits `spellcheck="true"`; only the row, their rendered view, opts out [S258, S257]. `I-spellcheck-off` checks the chips and the rendered rows only, so it passes [S261, S244]. Criterion 6 asks for spell check off on attribute lines (spec §4.3, §4.7), the corpus holds them (S-004, S-901), and editing one raw is an everyday edit, so this is a known bug under controller ruling 29 (amended): 1. The fix is one attribute, a line decoration with `spellcheck: "false"` on those lines [S20], and was not made within the budget. ProseMirror has the same defect (see there). In Chromium the Japanese composition and the `^` and `¨` dead keys give the oracle's bytes in prose, right after a chip and inside a voice block, 9 of 9; in WebKit the text insertion rows pass [S244]. The code is one `contentAttributes` line and `spellcheck="false"` on widget hosts; no line handles composition [S248, S249]. The composition part reads 3. Two parts could not be measured: composition in WebKit, which Playwright cannot drive, a gap for every variant [S244], and whether a webview draws the spelling marks (the manual checklist of spec §4.7). For them the paper evidence stands: the open Safari report #1403, the forum report of missing marks in a CodeMirror editor under Electron (Chromium 152), and the webview facts [S62, S63, S64, S65, S66]; it read 2. Without the known bug the weakest part would be these, and the cell 2; with it, 1.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 2.* The editable element is a `contenteditable` with real text nodes [S20]. Spell check is off by default (`spellcheck="false"` on the content element) and is turned on through `EditorView.contentAttributes`. A mark or line decoration with `spellcheck: "false"` opts a span out, and widgets are `contenteditable="false"` [S20]. In 6.43.15 the `EditContext` path runs only on Android, so WebView2 gets the `contenteditable` path [S20, S137]. Composition is actively fixed: 14 changelog entries on composition, IME or dead keys between 2025-11 and 2026-09, several about composing next to widgets and decoration boundaries, which is what live preview does at the caret [S20, S138, S139, S140]. Issues on dead keys: 5, none open; on Windows IME: 7, none open [S60, S61]. Open: #1403 (Korean IME in Safari, Enter duplicates a line break; the maintainer could not reproduce it) [S62]. A forum report saw no native marks in a CodeMirror editing host under Electron (Chromium 152) while a plain `contenteditable` had them; the maintainer saw spell checking work on Linux Chromium, and a second user's Chrome 152 report is unanswered [S63]. The maintainer's 2022 statement that Safari skips spell check of programmatically changed DOM was about iOS [S141], so whether WKWebView keeps marks under live-preview redraws is for the manual checklist. The issue counts are from the snapshot of 2026-04-15 (see Maintenance). Webview facts: see Webviews [S64, S65, S66]. Moderate custom work (attributes, care with decorations during composition), and the open reports are edge cases: 2.

**Long documents: 3→3.** The large file opens in Chromium in 38 ms, and a keystroke takes a median of 4.4 to 5.1 ms and a p95 of 15 to 16 ms at its start, middle and end; spec §4.8 asks for under 1 s and under 50 ms [S246]. The stress file opens in 80 ms, types at a p95 of 27 to 29 ms and scrolls to its end in 21 ms, with 50 MB of heap [S246]. WebKit, reported beside it (controller ruling 16): the large file opens in 55 ms and types at a p95 of 32 to 44 ms over the floor of about 30 ms that the measurement has there; `typingFrame` has a p95 of 7 to 14 ms and a median of 4 to 5 ms [S246]. 3.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 3.* CodeMirror renders only the viewport plus a margin and keeps height information for the whole document [S1]. The text is a tree of lines with cheap edits anywhere [S1], and the parser works incrementally, within time budgets, and stops beyond the viewport [S79]. Obsidian, a live-preview editor on CodeMirror 6, documents that its editor "supports huge documents with millions of lines" because it renders only what is visible [S80]. The cost to watch is in our code: decoration sets with block widgets (beat rows, callout heads) must be provided for the whole document from a state field, so they must be mapped through changes, not rebuilt per keystroke [S20, S47]. Open in the April snapshot: scrollbar and height-scaling issues on long documents (#1086, #415, #1278) [S81]. Stock virtualization with little glue: 3. The measured targets of spec §4.8 decide.

**Svelte 5 integration: 2→3.** All six integration checks pass in both engines: remount, a new context pushed in, the save and undo-redo shortcuts from the app's keymap, the external change that keeps the caret and the local undo, and the history marked on save [S245]. The paper's weakest part was clearing the history, for which the maintainer advised a new state. Measured, putting a fresh `history()` into its compartment takes 14 lines and passes `N-history` [S249, S248]. The external change is one transaction kept out of the history (15 lines); the app's keymap goes into `keymap.of` inside a compartment (28 lines); the context is a state field set by an effect; widgets mount and unmount the Svelte components [S249, S248]. No binding was needed. Stock mechanisms and little glue: 3, one more than on paper.

*On paper (2026-10-31): 2.* No official Svelte binding exists [S142]. `new EditorView({parent})` and `destroy()` mount and tear down in any element, so a runes component needs a few lines [S20]. The community wrapper `svelte-codemirror-editor` 2.1.0 is runes-only, but it rebuilds the state on every external `value`, losing selection and undo history, so it is not usable as is [S93]. Design-system components: `WidgetType.toDOM` and `destroy` map onto Svelte's `mount` and `unmount`, and `eq` and `updateDOM` avoid re-mounting [S20, S143]. Index and findings go in through state effects, compartments or `setDiagnostics`, without re-creating the editor [S3, S55]. One keymap: `keymap.of` takes a binding list built from data, `Mod-` is Ctrl or Cmd per platform, and default keymaps are opt-in [S20, S21]. External change: `@codemirror/merge` `diff()` gives the minimal changes, and `Transaction.addToHistory.of(false)` keeps them out of the undo history [S91, S3]; a probe kept the caret and let undo remove only the local edit [S133]. Undo history on save: `isolateHistory` marks a boundary, but there is no call that clears the history. The maintainer wrote "You can't clear history" and advised a new state [S21, S92], and `view.setState` redraws everything and re-initializes all view plugins [S20]. A probe cleared the history by reconfiguring a compartment that holds `history()` [S133]. The weakest part, clearing the history on save, needs a workaround: 2.

**Licence: pass→pass.** The licence report reads the dependency closure of the variant's packages: 13 packages, all MIT, nothing flagged [S247]. The closure covers the variant's packages only, not the app they share with every variant (Svelte, Vite) [S247]. The report does not look for network calls; the paper's probe found none in the runtime code [S133].

*On paper (2026-10-31): pass.* Every package is MIT [S112, S20]. A probe read MIT in all 22 packages of the resolved tree and found no network calls or install scripts in runtime code [S133]. Funding is voluntary; no feature needs a paid tier or a service [S113, S144]. Task 17's `pnpm licenses list` of the prototype replaces this.

**Maintenance: 2.** Releases: in 12 months 41 stable releases of `@codemirror/view` and 10 each of `@codemirror/state` and `@lezer/markdown` [S112]. API: no breaking change in the 6.x line since 6.0.0 (2022-06-08); a deprecated method keeps working [S20, S112]. Open issues in our areas were few in the GitHub snapshot of 2026-04-15: 12 open in `codemirror/dev`, one each on IME and Markdown, none on widgets or atomic ranges [S81, S33]. The material risk is backing. One maintainer wrote 120 of the 124 `view` commits in the period and 2,183 of its commits overall, against 37 for the next contributor [S125, S126]. Funding is voluntary, with four gold sponsors [S113, S144]. Asked about single-maintainer risk in 2026, the maintainer answered that he prefers working this way and sees no near-term reason for concern [S127]. The repositories moved to a self-hosted Forgejo on 2026-04-15, so the issue counts above are of that date [S127, S145]. One material risk that pinning and watching can manage: 2.

## ProseMirror/Tiptap

**Byte-stable Markdown: 1→1.** Scored as spec §4.4 fixes it: on the worse engine and on the best-effort column, with stock beside it. Both engines gave the same rows [S242].

Best effort: every unedited file is identical, 17 of 17, the CRLF file as its LF form, and every soft line break already in a file keeps its bytes; they sit in 6 of the 17 files, in wrapped prose, quotes, callouts and a table [S242, S250]. 57 of 60 edits match the oracle, 29 of 30 through `apply` and 28 of 30 through real input; every edit that is not a paste matches [S242]. The three mismatches are the two pastes, and neither changes a byte outside the pasted text: `paste-crlf`, through both drivers, saves the pasted line break as a paragraph break (one `\n` too many between the two pasted lines), and `paste-html`, through input, saves `.Pasted **rich**` instead of `. Stock, beside it: 5 of 17 unedited files and 8 of 60 edits are identical, and 54 of 77 rows per engine change bytes beyond the edited block [S242, S250]. On its own, stock would be 0.

Band 3 is out: three edits miss the oracle. The pastes alone would be band 2, confined to rare constructs with a named fix: each is one kind of paste, and the notes name a fix for each, plain-text paste through `transformPastedHTML` or `handlePaste` for the HTML, and a soft-break node in the schema and the parser, which a pasted line break would become [S251].

What decides band 1 is the class the notes call "the bytes between blocks have no node in a ProseMirror tree", measured in the same column beside the manifest edits [S251, S253]. Enter at the end of the last paragraph of a voice or a design callout, then typing: the editor makes a caption, and the serializer writes the whole callout again, so a line break inside its paragraph, which the edit did not touch, becomes a space; the design caption reloads as a paragraph, because design has no caption (variant M2; probe in both engines) [S253, S251]. An Enter that leaves an empty nested paragraph in a quote or a callout, with a save in between, does the same: the empty paragraph has no line of its own in Markdown, so the keystroke after the save writes the block again, and in a callout the empty caption saved in between reloads as a `—` paragraph, for every type [S251]. Those are everyday edits of a common construct, adding a paragraph to a voice, design or note block, and the probe shows them changing bytes beyond the edit inside the block: band 1 in the words of spec §4.4. The class has a named cause, not a named fix: fix round 1 fixed three variants with one set of rules, the reviews then found three more (M1, M2, O2), and controller ruling 26 stopped patching it; the notes conclude that byte stability at block boundaries is "bookkeeping that every new kind of boundary edit can break" [S251]. Rarer cases of the same column: M1 (a file without a final newline gets one), O2 (a callout that holds only its title leaks a blank line), a letter between a word and an `_` run, a letter that changes what a beat line is [S251]. Enter in an item of a tight list writes the list again too [S253], but that is partly an artefact of the spike: it binds no `splitListItem` from the stock `prosemirror-schema-list`, so Enter splits the item's paragraph instead of the item [S250].

So the mismatches are not confined to rare constructs with a named fix: not 2. Every unedited file is identical, and every mismatch found stays at block level: 1, not 0.

In its favour: every manifest edit other than the two pastes (insertions, deletions, links, note states, beat parameters, select-all, the external change, undo back to the start) reached span level in both engines, and so did the input checks' compositions and dead keys, in Chromium, where WebKit had text insertion only [S242, S244, S251]. It costs 1011 lines, 988 of them the save mapping, on top of the stock adapter's 2314 [S248]. If the separator class is left out because its cases are not manifest edits, the pastes alone make the cell 2 (see Measured scores).

*On paper (2026-10-31): 1.* The Markdown is not the document. Plain ProseMirror parses it with markdown-it into a tree, and `MarkdownSerializer` writes the whole tree again [S5]. Tiptap parses with `marked`, and `getMarkdown()` serializes the whole document on every call [S6]. The stock serializers normalize by design: the mark specs write only `*` and `**`, bullets default to `*`, exactly one blank line is written between blocks, and `esc()` escapes `[`, `]`, `_`, `*` and more, so `[[id]]` comes back escaped [S5]. Tiptap calls its Markdown extension an early release with edge cases and tells users to test their round trips [S7, S147]. Its tracker has open round-trip defects in our constructs: soft line breaks (#8136), marks around inline atoms (#8314), escaping that breaks round-trip stability (#8519, impact high) [S148, S149, S150]. A third-party benchmark found both forms byte-identical on 0 of 6 files [S9]. Probes on the corpus found 5 of 17 bodies identical for both forms, and those 5 were the empty bodies and one single line [S151, S152, S153]. The credible path is the best-effort column of spec §4.5. markdown-it block tokens carry `[line_begin, line_end]` maps, while inline tokens carry none [S8]; marked tokens carry `raw` [S154]; transactions carry step maps for document positions [S34]. No source shows inline source offsets or a span-level mapping inside an edited block [S8, S5]. Possible with heavy custom work: 1. Not 0, because the spec's best-effort path is credible; whether it reaches span level is what the prototype measures.

## Milkdown

Researched: @milkdown/kit, core, crepe, preset-commonmark, preset-gfm, transformer and plugin-listener 7.23.0 [S117]. Dossier: [S170]. Milkdown is a ProseMirror editor with a remark (mdast) Markdown layer; `@milkdown/kit` is the headless set and `@milkdown/crepe` a ready-made editor built on it [S171].

**Byte-stable Markdown: 1.** The document is a ProseMirror tree. remark parses the Markdown, and the serializer's `toString` runs `remark.stringify` over a new mdast tree built from the whole document, on every call [S10, S172]. The stringify options of mdast-util-to-markdown are one value per document (`bullet`, `rule`, `emphasis` and the rest), and `join` functions decide blank lines from mdast nodes, not from the source [S11]. The stock node schemas keep no source offsets or raw text (`code_block` keeps only its language) [S12], and the stock text handler escapes `[`, so `[[id]]` comes back escaped [S173]. Open serializer defects: #2505 (the list-order plugin corrupts the document root or throws), the open pull request #2487 (list items lose a leading block on save) and #2349 (autolink backslashes) [S13, S174]. In its favour: remark's mdast nodes, inline ones included, carry start and end offsets into the source, where markdown-it gives ProseMirror block line maps only [S8], and a custom node can emit a verbatim `html` node [S10, S57]. That is the most source information any tree editor here offers to a span-level best effort, but no source or example shows it in use [S38]. Probes: the stock round trip kept 2 of 13 non-empty bodies; a 60-line plugin set for links, callouts and a beat join kept 4 of 8 records [S175, S176]. Possible with heavy custom work: 1.

**Front matter untouched: 2.** No front matter support in the kit, Crepe or the presets; the request is open (#1712) [S24, S26]. Write mode: the API takes and returns strings (`defaultValueCtx`, `replaceAll`, `getMarkdown`), so the adapter can split the front matter off [S25]. A community route parses it with `remark-frontmatter` into a code node [S24]; a probe kept the front matter bytes that way [S177]. Source mode: Crepe has no whole-document source view; CodeMirror 6 is already a dependency for code blocks, and a 2024 community split-editing plugin exists [S26, S27, S100]. So Source mode needs a second component: at most 2; moderate work: 2.

**Inline link chips: 2.** No wikilink node or plugin exists in the packages, the repository or the community list [S41, S178]. The documented composables cover a custom inline node: `$remark` for parsing, `$nodeSchema` with `parseMarkdown` and `toMarkdown`, `$view` for a ProseMirror node view, `$prose` for plugins; the docs add that a real custom syntax needs a micromark extension [S38, S39]. Atom behaviour comes from ProseMirror underneath [S39]. The `[[` popover: `@milkdown/plugin-slash`'s `SlashProvider` positions content that the host supplies on a configurable trigger and does no item filtering, so filtering by place is ours [S40]. A probe round-tripped links with and without alias, inside emphasis and next to punctuation, through a text-visitor plugin and an inline atom [S176]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Callouts with their own look: 2.** Neither the packages nor Crepe have a callout node [S26]. A custom block node (`content: 'block+'` with a `kind` attribute) keeps the callout's text as editable paragraphs inside it, and decorations come in through `$prose` [S39]; a probe built such a node in a few lines [S176]. Showing the raw Markdown under the caret is not documented for 7.23.0: the 2022 hybrid-editing RFC was closed with its bold, italic, link and image items ticked, and `plugin-automd` turns typed Markdown into marks [S49]. Moderate custom work, as for ProseMirror: 2.

**Beat headings with attribute lines: 2.** A beat heading is the stock `heading`; the `key:: value` lines become one paragraph joined by inline `hardbreak` nodes that write a newline back [S12, S57]. The row as in `04-script.html` needs a custom node with a node view, or decorations through `$prose` [S39]. Findings reach plugins through slices and `editor.action` [S58, S25]. As for ProseMirror, a parameter edit stays in its node, and the bytes it saves are criterion 1's [S10]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Spell check and IME: 2.** The editing surface is ProseMirror's `contenteditable`, and composition is ProseMirror's (`view.composing`) [S70]. The maintainer points to `editorViewOptionsCtx` attributes for the `spellcheck` attribute, and a node's `toDOM` sets per-node attributes [S49, S38]; a probe found no `spellcheck` in Milkdown's own sources [S178]. Milkdown's own plugins respect composition (`syncHeadingIdPlugin` returns early while composing), and the GFM preset carries a Safari IME workaround [S85, S179]. Past IME reports are closed and none is open [S71]. Two risks are Milkdown's own. Its source uses regex lookbehind (in the commonmark preset's line-break plugin, so not only in Crepe), and a Tauri 2 app with Crepe failed with "invalid group specifier name" on an embedded WebKit without lookbehind (#2190, closed the same day) [S57, S72]. Crepe's list-item component has open typing-order bugs (#2506, #2501); they are Crepe-only and set aside (see Scoring rules) [S13]. ProseMirror's open composition issues apply as well (see ProseMirror/Tiptap) [S69]. Webview facts: see Webviews [S64, S65, S66]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Long documents: 2.** The whole document is rendered as DOM; the docs and the Crepe features show no virtualization option [S58, S171, S26]. Work on every change: `syncHeadingIdPlugin` walks the whole document after each change, and the listener serializes the whole document after a 200 ms debounce [S85, S86]. A user reports that `replaceAll` froze the UI on a "relatively large document" (#2396, a user statement) [S87]. Earlier slowness on ~150 KB documents and a quadratic line-break plugin were fixed [S180]. A probe parsed the 2.5 MB stress body in 10.4 s, growing faster than the input [S181]; that is a warning for a prototype, not a score. Moderate work (drop the heading-id plugin, no serialize per change): 2.

**Svelte 5 integration: 2.** The official integrations are React and Vue; the Svelte recipe is a vanilla `use:` action without runes or teardown [S100, S101], and no community Svelte binding was found [S182]. `Editor.make()…create()` and `await editor.destroy()` mount and tear down [S25]. Node views are raw ProseMirror constructors through `$view`, so Svelte `mount` and `unmount` are ours [S39]. Milkdown's own node views are Vue 3, and `@milkdown/kit` depends on `@milkdown/components`, so Vue is installed with the kit [S117, S27]. Host data goes in through slices and `editor.action` [S58, S25]. Keymap: one slice per command with `Mod`; no hook for a host-owned keymap [S102]. External change: `replaceAll` replaces the whole document [S103]. `@milkdown/plugin-diff` (first published 2026-04-01) gives `computeDocDiff` [S104], and the patch pattern of the open request #2396 [S87] kept the caret in a probe [S183]. History: `prosemirror-history` is re-exported (`closeHistory`, `undoDepth`); the only reset found is re-creating the state [S105, S103]. Open: `destroy()` throws when a Crepe code block's CodeMirror failed to start (#2507) [S13]. The weakest parts need moderate work, ProseMirror's plus Milkdown's own layer: 2.

**Licence: pass.** Every `@milkdown/*` package is MIT [S117, S118]. The direct dependencies are MIT except `dompurify` (MPL-2.0 OR Apache-2.0) [S27]. A probe walked 210 transitive packages: 206 MIT, plus dompurify, BSD-2-Clause, BSD-3-Clause and ISC, all permissive [S184]. No paid tier: the docs call it "open source and fully free" [S58]. Network: the only `fetch` calls are in Crepe's AI providers (OpenAI and Anthropic hosts), and the AI feature is off by default; a kit-only editor does not include them [S26]. The "telemetry" inspector is in-process and off by default [S185].

**Maintenance: 2.** Releases: 16 in 12 months, and 260 commits on `main`, 125 of them by people [S117, S186, S129]. Backing: one maintainer wrote nearly all of it (2,474 commits; the next person has 14), with one npm account, GitHub Sponsors and no company [S117, S129, S118]. That is CodeMirror 6's risk, and Milkdown also rests on ProseMirror's core, whose single maintainer and open composition, WebKit and decoration issues come with it [S114, S69]. Milkdown's own tracker is small (36 open issues, 8 labelled bug, none on IME), but the open serializer defects of criterion 1 (#2505, pull request #2487) sit in the commonmark preset that a Theos editor would use [S239, S71, S13]. Output: changes that alter the Markdown ship in patch and minor releases (7.22.2, 7.22.3, 7.23.0), with no stated breaking-change policy, and the packages take `remark`, `remark-stringify` and `unified` by caret ranges [S130, S13, S172]; that is the risk every tree editor shares (see Scoring rules). #2516, saved output that changed in 7.23.0, needs Crepe's LaTeX feature, and its report names Milkdown's own new inline-math guard as the likely cause; the newer `mdast-util-to-markdown` is only listed beside it [S13]. As a Crepe-only issue it is set aside, as in criterion 6. The risks stack, Milkdown's layer on ProseMirror's, but each is one that pinning and watching can manage, as for CodeMirror 6 and ProseMirror/Tiptap: 2.

## Lexical

Researched: lexical 0.53.0 with @lexical/markdown, @lexical/mdast, @lexical/history, @lexical/extension, @lexical/rich-text, @lexical/list, @lexical/link and @lexical/react 0.53.0 [S29]. Dossier: [S187].

**Byte-stable Markdown: 1.** The document is an `EditorState` tree, Markdown is an import and export conversion, and nodes carry no source offsets [S14, S15]. Import runs line by line through regex transformers; export rebuilds the whole string from the tree and joins blocks with `\n` [S15]. The package keeps some syntax in NodeState (list marker, code fence and meta, hard-break style) and has a `shouldPreserveNewLines` mode that skips normalization and escapes less [S15]; emphasis markers are not in that list [S15]. Open export-fidelity issues: nested-list indentation is rewritten (#2213), tables lose their newlines (#9323), and bold or italic text that ends in punctuation exports as Markdown that imports as plain text (#9660) [S16, S17]. The experimental `@lexical/mdast` "stays close to the source, though not exact" [S188]. Probes: the default mode kept 5 of 17 corpus bodies and the preserve mode 14 of 17, and a one-word edit in preserve mode changed no line beyond those the unedited round trip already changed [S189]. Those are the best tree-editor numbers on the corpus, but they are probes, and preserve mode still writes every block again. Span level needs heavy custom work (transformers that keep every spelling, or each block's source kept in NodeState): 1.

**Front matter untouched: 2.** No Lexical package has a front-matter concept, so the adapter splits it off, which the string API allows [S15, S188]. The docs describe no per-range protection inside one editor: only the global `setEditable`, TextNode `token` mode (deleted all at once) and decorators [S14, S28, S190]. A Source mode inside the same component would need that protection. A plain-text editor (`@lexical/plain-text`) is available as a second instance [S29], and a probe found that a file stored as lines of text returns its exact text for 17 of 17 corpus files [S189]. Second component: at most 2; moderate work: 2.

**Inline link chips: 2.** An inline `DecoratorNode` is the chip. Its DOM is `contenteditable="false"`, the host renders into it through `registerDecoratorListener`, and by default the caret can step over it and select it [S28, S42]; whether stepping over takes one press or two (select, then pass) is not documented, and `isKeyboardSelectable` can be overridden [S42]. Markdown import goes through a text-match transformer, and export writes the decorator's `getTextContent()`, so the chip keeps `[[id|text]]` verbatim; the exporter applies no text format to decorators, so emphasis around a chip is lost [S15, S191]. The `[[` popover: the typeahead plugin and its trigger matcher are in `@lexical/react` only, so a Svelte app builds its own [S43]. Composition next to token and segmented nodes was fixed (#6296); one caret position is still outside the heuristic (#8507, closed as not reproducible) [S192]. Open: the caret at the start of an inline decorator with up and down keys (#5312) [S44]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Callouts with their own look: 2.** A custom `ElementNode` gives the callout its own DOM (`createDOM`, `updateDOM`, and `getDOMSlot` for wrappers and accessory nodes) while its children stay editable in place [S42, S28]; `@lexical/mdast` offers blockquotes as block containers as an opt-in [S188]. The docs read show no decoration layer: a range is marked with nodes (`MarkNode`, or a `TextNode` subclass like `HashtagNode` made by a node transform), so developer words become nodes in the tree [S50, S42]; the hashtag package is the stock precedent. Lexical has no live-preview feature; its only Markdown example is a split view [S51]. Showing the raw title under the caret means swapping nodes or classes on selection change [S42]. Open: Markdown directive syntax (#8903) [S16]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Beat headings with attribute lines: 2.** `## …` imports as a `HeadingNode` and `key:: value` lines as paragraph text [S15]. The row needs a custom element node with its own DOM [S42]. Findings reach a block through `registerMutationListener` and `getElementByKey`, and the experimental `BlockIdExtension` gives each block an id that survives undo and reloads [S42, S59]. A parameter edit stays in its node; the bytes it saves are criterion 1's [S15]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Spell check and IME: 2.** The root is a native `contenteditable` with real DOM text [S42]. Core sets `spellcheck="false"` only on code-format text, and custom nodes set their own attributes in `createDOM`; no per-range switch is documented [S42, S28]. Until 0.53.0 every programmatic text write dropped Chromium's spelling underlines for the whole text node. The fix (#9354) shipped in 0.53.0 on 2026-10-28, three days before the read date [S73, S59]. On WebKit (Safari, iOS, WebKitGTK) the full write is kept on purpose, so that a stale underline is dropped [S42]. Lexical runs its own composition handling, and the dead-key and German-layout reports found are fixed (#9289 in 0.53.0, #7026, #8697) [S191, S74, S193, S194]; 0.53.0 also fixes cancelled Pinyin compositions in desktop WebKit (#9362) [S195]. Open: committing a Korean composition with a mouse click scrolls the viewport (#8834), and the Korean caret is misplaced in some fonts (#4753) [S75, S196]. Composition code branches on `IS_SAFARI || IS_APPLE_WEBKIT`, read from the user agent and `navigator.platform`; whether Tauri's WKWebView and WebKitGTK match is unknown, and WebKitGTK is not Apple [S42]. Webview facts: see Webviews [S64, S65, S66]. Moderate custom work: 2.

**Long documents: 2.** There is no built-in virtualization, and the maintainer notes that "Most people use lexical with much smaller documents" [S88]. A contributor's benchmark in real Chromium built 107k nodes (1,000 pages) in 146 ms, 225 ms with transforms and history, plus 269 ms of reflow; a user's 35 MB document took about 30 s to import and spent its typing time in browser layout [S88]. Reconciliation is batched in a microtask [S197]. History keeps one `EditorState` per entry, with no depth limit by default [S89]. Open: slow Safari performance with large character counts (#5683) [S44]. Whole-DOM rendering; the large file is within the benchmark's range, and the stress file needs care: 2.

**Svelte 5 integration: 1.** No official Svelte package, but an official Svelte 5 example in the repository (SvelteKit, 0.53.0) sets the root element in `$effect` and keeps Lexical's `$`-prefixed functions out of `.svelte` files, where they clash with Svelte's `$` namespace [S107]. The community `svelte-lexical` pins Lexical 0.40 to 0.41, and an app must resolve exactly one copy of each Lexical package, so it does not combine with 0.53.0 [S108, S198, S109]. Components render through the decorator listener and `mount`; rendering inside `createDOM` broke on nested reconciles [S198]. History is the strong part: `CLEAR_HISTORY_COMMAND` clears undo and redo, and the host can own the `HistoryState` [S89]. The keymap: core dispatches `UNDO_COMMAND` on Ctrl/Cmd+Z itself; commands can be intercepted at a higher priority, and a keyboard-shortcut extension is experimental [S42, S190, S191]. The weakest part decides: an external change applied as a minimal edit that keeps the local undo. Lexical has no diff or minimal-edit API: `$convertFromMarkdownString` clears the root and moves the selection to the start, and `setEditorState` replaces the whole state [S15, S106]. Its history keeps one `EditorState` per entry [S89], so undoing the local edit after an external change restores a state without the external change. Meeting the oracle of spec §4.6 (undo removes the local edit, the external change stays) needs a node-level diff and a rewritten history stack. Lexical's own sources suggest how. The host owns the `HistoryState` [S89], and the experimental `BlockIdExtension` gives each block an id that survives undo [S59]. On an external change the host would diff the blocks of the re-imported file against the live document, without source offsets to anchor the diff [S14]. It would apply the changed blocks in an update kept out of history, and then rewrite every undo and redo entry: load its stored `EditorState` into a headless editor, replace the same blocks by id, and store the result. That is heavy custom work, not moderate. It replaces the history's own rule (undo puts back a stored snapshot) with host code; it rests on an experimental API; no source shows it working; and each external change costs one rewrite per entry, with no depth limit by default [S89]. In CodeMirror 6, undo applies inverted change sets [S21], and ProseMirror's history takes a change kept out of it with `addToHistory: false` and has fixed remote changes corrupting it [S98]; both need one flag on a diff. Heavy custom work: 1.

**Licence: pass.** MIT, copyright Meta Platforms [S29, S119]. A probe read MIT in all 19 packages of the core set (75 with `@lexical/mdast`) and found no network calls in their sources [S199]. The website's analytics are site code, not a package; no feature needs a paid tier [S59, S119].

**Maintenance: 2.** Very active: 15 releases in 12 months, and 951 commits by 151 authors, under Meta's organization with 6 npm maintainers [S29, S119]. Open issues by label in our areas are few (markdown 5, composition 3, decorators 3) [S44]. The material risk is API churn. Lexical is 0.x and "breaking changes ship in minor releases" [S109]; 12 of the last 15 releases have breaking entries (33 lines), and several APIs we would use are experimental (`@lexical/mdast`, `KeyboardShortcut`, `BlockIdExtension`) [S59]. Output changes too: 0.53.0 changed how export escapes literal text (#9481) and how import unescapes it (#9396) [S59], the risk every tree editor shares (see Scoring rules). Each release documents its migrations [S109]. Pinning and migrating is manageable: 2.

## Monaco

Researched: monaco-editor 0.57.0 [S120]. Dossier: [S200].

**Byte-stable Markdown: 3.** The document is a piece-tree text buffer over the raw string; Markdown is only tokenized for colour [S18]. Edits are ranges on that buffer, change events carry `rangeOffset` and `rangeLength`, and positions map to source offsets [S19, S18]. Normalizations: a file with mixed line ends is rewritten to one kind, a lone CR becomes the model's line end, and a BOM is held apart and written back only on request [S18]. The corpus has no mixed-ending file, and its CRLF file is expected back as LF, which `getValue` can return [S19]. Every option that changes typed or pasted text (`trimAutoWhitespace`, auto-closing, `formatOnPaste` and the rest) has an off switch [S19]. A probe read LF, CRLF, trailing whitespace, U+00A0, a decomposed é and an emoji back unchanged, and an edit came out as an exact splice [S201]. Stock with configuration: 3.

**Front matter untouched: 2.** Source mode comes naturally, because the whole file is one model; protection does not. Read-only exists only for the whole editor, there is no read-only or editable range, and no event can veto an edit before it lands [S19]. The request "Provide ability to block editing specific lines" (#953) has been open since 2018, with 152 reactions and no member reply found [S30]. The workarounds are key interception ("There are many special cases to deal with.") or a community plugin last pushed in 2023 [S30, S31]. So the credible route is a second component: the front matter in a read-only element, or in a second `readOnly` instance above the body [S19]. At most 2; moderate work: 2.

**Inline link chips: 1.** Monaco can style real text (`inlineClassName`) and inject view-only text before or after a range (`InjectedTextOptions.content`, a single-line plain string, with `cursorStops`). But no decoration replaces or hides part of a line, no inline DOM node takes space in the text flow (content widgets float beside it), and there are no atomic ranges [S19]. Hiding parts of the text has been requested since 2016 (#45) [S45]; a search for "atomic ranges" in the tracker found nothing [S46]. A chip that shows title and kind glyph over its hidden `[[id]]`, stepped over and deleted as one unit, would rest on undocumented CSS and on intercepting keys with `onKeyDown` and `preventDefault` [S30]. The `[[` list is Monaco's own suggest widget, with no field for DOM, so LinkAutocomplete cannot replace it [S19]. Dangling links can show as markers [S19]. Possible only with heavy custom work: 1.

**Callouts with their own look: 1.** Whole-line looks are stock (`isWholeLine` classes, margin and line decorations, block classes, fonts and line height per decoration), and the text stays plain model text, editable in place [S19]. Developer words fit `inlineClassName` decorations [S19]. But a callout's Markdown cannot leave the screen: nothing hides the `> ` marks or `[!voice]` within a line, and whole lines hide only through `setHiddenAreas`, which is not in the public typings [S19, S52]. The README says only `monaco.d.ts` is versioned and everything else "might break with any release" [S53], so a rendered head off the caret needs a view zone plus a private API. Open: text with a larger font size wraps wrongly (vscode #288873), and decoration line height is not applied after wrapping (vscode #249967) [S54]. Heavy custom work or a private API: 1.

**Beat headings with attribute lines: 1.** Beat lines are plain model lines, so a parameter edit changes only its line [S18]. The row can be a view zone holding a mounted component between lines, and a finding can be a marker or a glyph [S19]. But the raw heading and `key:: value` lines stay visible beside the row unless they are hidden, which again needs the private `setHiddenAreas` (whole lines only) or folding tricks [S52, S19], and the `## Beat ` prefix cannot be hidden within its line [S19]. Heavy custom work or a private API: 1.

**Spell check and IME: 0.** The criterion asks for prose as real text in a `contenteditable` with native spell check, "not a hidden textarea". Monaco's input is never `contenteditable`: it is a hidden `<textarea>` or an `EditContext`, both created with `spellcheck="false"`, and the typings have no spell-check option [S76, S19]. The EditContext explainer states that it makes "the native spellchecking capabilities of the browser unreachable" [S77]. The request for the browser's spell checker (#1977) has been open since 2020 without a member reply [S78]. A third-party JavaScript spell checker with markers exists, but it is not native (no OS dictionaries or suggestions) [S202]. IME is handled, with open issues on both input paths (for example #4805, decorations not updating during Windows IME input) [S203], but the first requirement has no credible path: 0. The window-level webview facts (IME window placement, the dead-key panic in tao) apply to Monaco too [S64, S204].

**Long documents: 2.** Rendering keeps a window of lines [S52], and the size limits lie far above the stress file (tokenization stops above 20 MB or 300,000 lines) [S18]. Prose needs a proportional font with word wrap. The default `simple` wrapping is described as correct only for monospace fonts and scripts with equal-width glyphs, and `advanced` "might cause freezes for large files" [S52]. A hang report for advanced wrapping on large files is open (vscode #117106), and so is a memory report (#5311) [S90, S205]. Stock virtualization with a wrapping cost for prose: 2.

**Svelte 5 integration: 1.** No official binding; `monaco.editor.create` and `dispose` mount and tear down [S53, S19]. The community `svelte-monaco` (last release 2025-04) loads Monaco through `@monaco-editor/loader`, whose default is a CDN, and calls `setValue` on changes [S110, S111], which clears the history and all decorations [S18]. Components: view zones and content widgets take any element, but injected text is a string, so a chip cannot be a Svelte component [S19]. Keymap: `KeyMod.CtrlCmd`, `addCommand` and `addKeybindingRule`; whether a null command removes a built-in binding is not documented [S19, S52]. The weakest parts decide. An external change that keeps the local undo has no clean path: `applyEdits` stays off the undo stack, with a warning of "dire consequences on the undo stack", while `pushEditOperations` and `executeEdits` put the change on the stack [S19]. Nothing clears the history except `setValue` or a new model [S19, S18]. Workers need `MonacoEnvironment` and, under Tauri's CSP, untested `worker-src` and runtime `<style>` handling [S124, S206, S207]. Heavy custom work: 1.

**Licence: pass.** MIT, Microsoft [S120, S121]. The runtime dependencies are `marked` (MIT) and `dompurify` (MPL-2.0 OR Apache-2.0) [S120, S122]. The bundled `codicon.ttf` has no notice entry; the codicons repository licenses its content CC-BY-4.0, a permissive licence that asks for attribution [S121, S123], and a probe's core-only bundle emitted no font [S208]. The standalone telemetry service is a no-op, and the package's network calls are in the JSON language service and the optional LSP client, which a prose editor does not import [S124, S121]. CDN loading is a wrapper default, not Monaco's [S111]. No feature needs a paid tier [S124].

**Maintenance: 1.** Backing is strong: Microsoft, with the editor generated from VS Code's sources [S120, S53]. Releases are sparse: four stable releases in 12 months with a 242-day gap, and 143 of 218 commits by dependabot [S120, S46]. API: 0.x, only `monaco.d.ts` is versioned, and the last four releases brought breaking changes (0.53.0 deprecated the AMD build, 0.55.0 moved the language namespaces, 0.56.0 reorganised the ESM entry points) [S53, S131]. The areas we need are not being worked on: native spell check (#1977, since 2020) and read-only ranges (#953, since 2018) have no member replies [S78, S30], and Monaco bug reports are asked to reproduce in VS Code first [S209]. Staying on it means workarounds kept for good: 1.

## Webviews

These facts come from the webview dossier and hold for every `contenteditable` candidate alike: CodeMirror 6, ProseMirror/Tiptap, Milkdown and Lexical [S64]. They are risks for P11 to check in the real Tauri shell, not differences between candidates.

Spell check
- WebView2: spell check is on by default; `spellcheck="false"` switches it off per element; WebView2 has no API for it [S210, S65, S211]. A `contenteditable` focused by script gets no underlines until the user clicks into it (#5728, open; the report names ProseMirror and Tiptap apps) [S65]. The spelling language follows the environment `Language`, which wry sets from the Windows UI language, and ignores `lang` [S212, S213, S214]. Runtime 106 sent typed text to a Microsoft service; runtime 109 fixed it [S215, S216].
- WKWebView: continuous spell checking is off unless the app's defaults hold `WebContinuousSpellCheckingEnabled`, which WebKit reads once at first use, so the app must set it before the webview is created (Tauri #7705, open) [S217, S218]. The `spellcheck` attribute then gates each element [S219]. Smart quote, dash and text substitutions follow the system settings and could rewrite typed text, which touches criterion 1; one Tauri report saw quotes replaced (#12286) [S217, S220].
- WebKitGTK: off by default. The app must call both `set_spell_checking_enabled` and `set_spell_checking_languages`, which neither wry nor Tauri does; they are reachable through `with_webview` [S221, S222, S223, S224]. The engine is Enchant, and `lang` is not consulted [S225].
- Tauri itself has no spell-check switch and no spelling menu item [S212, S224, S226].

IME and dead keys
- WebView2: in a `contenteditable` the first CJK character of a composition can be lost and Chinese punctuation can need two presses, while a `<textarea>` on the same page is correct (#5625, open; worse while the main thread is busy) [S66]. CJK IMEs can freeze on the first focus of a field that holds text; the report names ProseMirror and TipTap (Tauri #15436, open) [S161]. Composition windows jump to the screen corner after a resize or a window switch [S227, S228].
- Windows dead keys: a German-layout dead key can panic tao, Tauri's windowing layer (#1408, open, one report) [S204]. Whether WebView2 sends composition events for dead keys today has no current source; the only statement is from 2016 [S229].
- WKWebView: dead keys start a composition, and the `keypress` after it carries the dead key's data (WebKit 167880) [S230]. The order of `compositionend` and `keydown` was corrected by a flag in April 2026 and reverted in June 2026 [S231, S232].
- WebKitGTK: wry turns IME preedit off on every webview, so a composition may arrive as `compositionend` without `compositionstart` (code reading, not run) [S212, S233]. IME window placement after scrolling (Tauri #11412) and the Compose key on KDE (#15859) are open [S234, S235].
- Playwright has no composition API: CDP `Input.imeSetComposition` works on Chromium only, WebKit gets text insertion only, and neither goes through the OS input method [S236, S237, S238].

## Top two

- Shared. Both are ProseMirror underneath, so ProseMirror's one maintainer and its open composition, WebKit and decoration issues come with either [S114, S69]. ## Measured scores

The prototypes of the top two were measured by the P10 harness: Task 17's run of 2026-11-03 on commit 6b22b2b, with the machine and the browser versions in each report [S242, S243, S244, S245, S246, S247, S248]. The cells of the top two show `<paper>→<measured>`, and the totals use the measured score. The reports and the prototype notes [S249, S250, S251] are the harness's measurements, not researcher probes. The rules are those of spec §4.4, fixed before any result existed. Two readings were made at scoring time, after the results were known, and are recorded as controller rulings 29 and 30 (P10 ledger, 2026-11-04); they are marked below. This is how the rules were applied.

- **Criterion 1** is scored on the worse of the two engines and, for ProseMirror, on the best-effort column, with stock beside it. Both engines gave the same rows for every variant [S242].
- **Criteria 3–6 and 8** use the scale of spec §4.4 for what the prototype achieved: 3 = works as defined with stock features and little glue · 2 = works with moderate custom code · 1 = works only in part within the budget, or with known bugs · 0 = not achieved and no credible path found. Spec §4.4 sets no measured rule for criterion 2, so it uses the same scale with the cap of spec §4.3: Source mode in the same component up to 3, a second component at most 2. The lines per feature of the adapter-size report are evidence for 2 against 3, not a threshold [S248].
- **The weakest part decides**, as on paper (Scoring rules). A part the harness measured is scored on the measurement, and open issues it did not trigger are named as risks. A part it could not measure (composition in WebKit, the spelling marks a webview draws; spec §4.7) keeps its paper evidence.
- **Known bugs** (band 1), a reading made at scoring time. Controller ruling 29 (amended 2026-11-04): "a recorded defect counts as a 'known bug' only where it hits a construct the corpus holds, or an everyday edit on such a construct (e.g. Enter at the end of a callout paragraph); limits in constructs the corpus does not hold are named in the comparison but not scored — one standard for both editors". So a recorded defect lowers a cell to 1 when the editor does something wrong, or nothing, on a construct the corpus holds or on an everyday edit of such a construct. A missing convenience, or a limit in a construct the corpus does not hold (both editors have one such `Design note:` case), is named for both editors and lowers neither. This reading decided ProseMirror's criteria 3 and 4 and, with controller ruling 32, criterion 6 of both.
- **Bytes are scored once** (Scoring rules). A check that fails in ProseMirror's stock column only because it compares the saved bytes (`F-source-mode`, `F-link-popover-escape`, `N-external-caret`, `N-keymap-undo-redo`, the composition and insertion rows) counts under criterion 1. ProseMirror's criteria 2–8 are scored on the best-effort column, the form that would have to be built for G5.
- **Criterion 7** uses the targets of spec §4.8 on Chromium's `typing` numbers (controller ruling 16). WebKit's are reported beside them: the way `typing` ends in WebKit puts a floor of about 30 ms under every keystroke [S246]. The spec gives no number for "the stress file only has to stay usable". A reading made at scoring time (controller ruling 30) takes it as: it opens, typing lands, and the editor does not hang; in the run both stress files also scrolled to their end without an error [S246]. This reading decided ProseMirror's criterion 7.
- **Criterion 9** follows the licence report; **criterion 10** keeps its paper score.
- **Budget** (spec §4.4): one implementation task and one fix round per feature; what was still missing after that is scored on what works.

Result:
- Knockouts by measurement: none. ProseMirror's stock column alone would be knocked out on criterion 1 (5 of 17 unedited files identical); the best-effort column is not [S242].
- Totals: CodeMirror 6 42→42, ProseMirror/Tiptap 33→32. The order does not change: CodeMirror 6 leads by 10 points (9 on paper). Controller ruling 32 (2026-11-04) lowered criterion 6 of both from the first measured 2 to 1; the first measured totals were 44 and 34.
- Up for CodeMirror 6: Svelte 5 integration (2→3). Up for ProseMirror/Tiptap: front matter untouched (2→3), long documents (2→3) and Svelte 5 integration (2→3). Down for both: spell check and IME (2→1). Down for ProseMirror/Tiptap: inline link chips (2→1) and callouts with their own look (2→1). The other cells of both keep their paper score.
- The next-ranked alternative is still Milkdown (33), then Lexical (31); both were scored on paper only.

The readings Jann may overrule, and what each would move:
- ProseMirror criterion 1 at 2, if the separator class is left out because its cases are not manifest edits, so that the pastes alone decide (band 2): ProseMirror +3.
- ProseMirror criteria 3 and 4 at 2, if links shown as text in raw titles and attribute lines, and Enter at the end of a callout paragraph, count as limits rather than defects: +2 each.
- ProseMirror criterion 2 at 2, if losing the undo history at every mode switch counts against "the same component": −2.
- ProseMirror criterion 7 at 2, if "usable" means a latency bound that a p95 of 181 to 228 ms misses: −1.
- Criterion 6 at 2 for both, if spell check on the raw attribute lines counts as a limit rather than a known bug: +2 each; at 3 for both, if the parts the harness could not measure are also left to P11: +4 each.
- "Known bugs" read broadly for both, so that every recorded limit lowers its cell, also in constructs the corpus does not hold (CodeMirror 6 criteria 3, 4 and 5; ProseMirror criterion 5): CodeMirror 6 36, ProseMirror/Tiptap 30.

With every one of these readings turned ProseMirror's way (criteria 1, 3 and 4 at 2, criterion 6 at 3 for both), ProseMirror/Tiptap has 43 and CodeMirror 6 46; with "known bugs" read broadly for both, 30 and 36. The order holds under each reading applied to both editors alike. A mixed standard would flip it: every reading turned ProseMirror's way (43) against CodeMirror 6 with "known bugs" read broadly and criterion 6 at 3 (40). That is why one standard is applied to both.

The records are not equally probed. ProseMirror's best-effort column went through three review rounds that probed Enter at block boundaries; CodeMirror 6's Enter in callouts and lists got no such probing. CodeMirror 6 has no recorded defect there partly because nobody looked, though its Enter cannot rewrite a block: it inserts a newline into the text, which is the file. It runs the other way too: CodeMirror 6's criterion 5 limits come partly from the shared `BeatRow` component, which lets no unknown key be edited from the row (`isBeatKey`) [S257, S249]. ProseMirror mounts the same component, but its notes do not record that limit [S250]. Neither cell is scored on it.

For the decision record, not scored by any criterion: ProseMirror's stock schema holds a soft line break as a space, so its Write mode cannot show a line break of the file as written (spec §3, Jann's choice for P10) [S251, S250]. The soft-break node named under criterion 1 would fix this and the line-end paste alike.
